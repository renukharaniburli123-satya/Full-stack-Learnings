  1. COMPONENTS
    • Waste Generation: The starting point where items become unwanted trash in homes, schools, offices, and industries.
    • On-site Storage and Handling: Keeping waste safely in bins, bags, or containers at the source before workers pick it up.
    • Collection: Gathering the stored garbage from properties and loading it into specialized collection trucks.
    • Transfer and Transport: Moving waste from smaller collection trucks into larger trailers at transfer stations for long-distance hauling.
    • Processing and Recovery: Sorting materials through Recycling and Composting or converting non-recyclable items into energy.
    • Disposal: The final step of burying leftover, residual waste safely inside engineered Landfills.
  2. TYPES OF SOLID WASTE
    • Landfilling:Burying waste in specifically designed, controlled sites. Modern landfills use protective liners and pipes to stop liquids from leaking into the ground water.
    • Incineration: Burning waste at very high temperatures. This process reduces the total volume of trash and can destroy harmful germs, often while generating heat or electricity.
    • Recycling: Collecting used materials like glass, paper, metal, and plastic and processing them to make new products. This saves natural resources and energy.
    • Composting: Allowing organic waste—such as food scraps and garden trimmings—to naturally decompose. This creates a rich, safe fertilizer for soil.
    • Waste-to-Energy (WTE): Converting non-recyclable waste materials into usable heat, fuel, or electricity through combustion or other thermal processes.
  3. CATEGORIES OF WASTES
    • Residential/Domestic Waste: Food leftovers, paper, plastics, and packaging discarded by households.
    • Commercial and Industrial Waste: Metals, glass, chemical residues, and packaging from businesses and factories.
    • Agricultural Waste: Crop residues, husk, manure, and field litter.
    • Construction and Demolition Debris: Concrete, wood, bricks, and metal scraps from building sites.
